01 / Introduction

Greedy-Choice Property and Optimal Substructure

Two important properties used to design and understand efficient algorithms for optimization problems.

How can we make a good decision now and still guarantee the best possible overall solution?
02 / Context

Why Do These Properties Matter?

Optimization problems involve finding the best solution while following certain rules or limitations.

Property 1

Greedy-Choice Property

A globally optimal solution can be reached by making a locally optimal choice at each step.

Property 2

Optimal Substructure

An optimal solution can be constructed from optimal solutions to smaller subproblems.

The first property focuses on making a choice. The second focuses on how smaller solutions form a larger one.
03 / Example

Greedy-Choice Property in Action

Consider a fractional knapsack problem. A bag can carry 25 kg, and we want to maximize the value of the items inside it.

Available items

Gold 10 kg · $60/kg
Silver 20 kg · $50/kg
Bronze 30 kg · $40/kg

Greedy rule: Choose the available item with the highest value per kilogram first.

Greedy selection

Take 10 kg of gold and then 15 kg of silver.

$600 + $750 = $1,350

All 25 kg of capacity is used.

04 / Method

How the Greedy-Choice Property Works

1

Define the goal.
Maximize the total value of items in the bag.

2

Choose a local rule.
Prefer the item with the highest value per kilogram.

3

Make the best immediate choice.
Take the available gold first because it has the highest value density.

4

Continue with the remaining capacity.
Take silver until the bag reaches 25 kg.

05 / Evidence and Proof

Why Does the Greedy Choice Work?

Exchange Argument

In the fractional knapsack problem, suppose a solution takes some lower-value-density material while a higher-value-density item is still available.

We can replace a small amount of the lower-density material with the same weight of the higher-density material. This increases the total value without exceeding the bag's capacity.

Therefore, choosing the highest value per kilogram first is safe for this problem.

Highest value per kilogram
↓
Replace lower-value material when possible
↓
No worse solution is needed

This proof applies to the fractional knapsack problem. The same greedy rule does not always work for 0/1 knapsack.

06 / Example

Optimal Substructure in Action

Imagine finding the shortest route from Town A to Town C through Town B.

Town A → Town C
↓
Subproblem 1

Find the shortest route from A to B.

Subproblem 2

Find the shortest route from B to C.

↓
Combine both shortest routes

Main idea

If the route from A to C through B is optimal, its A-to-B and B-to-C portions must also be shortest paths for their respective endpoints.

07 / Method

How Optimal Substructure Works

1

Identify the main problem.
Find the shortest route from A to C.

2

Break it into smaller problems.
Find the shortest routes from A to B and from B to C.

3

Solve the subproblems optimally.
Find the best route for each smaller section.

4

Combine the solutions.
Join the shortest sections to form the full route.

08 / Evidence and Proof

Why Is It Optimal Substructure?

Proof by Contradiction

Assume the route from A to C is the shortest route through B, but the section from A to B is not the shortest possible route.

Then a shorter route from A to B must exist. Replacing the original section with that shorter route would make the entire A-to-C route shorter.

This contradicts the assumption that the original route was the shortest.

Therefore, an optimal solution must contain optimal solutions to its subproblems.

If a smaller part of an optimal solution could be improved, then the whole solution could be improved too.
09 / Comparison

Greedy Choice vs. Optimal Substructure

Choosing

Greedy-Choice Property

Focuses on making the best immediate choice that can safely lead to an optimal overall solution.

Question: Which choice should we make now?

Building

Optimal Substructure

Focuses on constructing an optimal solution from optimal solutions to smaller subproblems.

Question: How do smaller optimal solutions form the whole?

Remember

Greedy algorithms commonly rely on both properties. Optimal substructure alone does not guarantee that a greedy strategy will work; the greedy-choice property must also hold.

10 / Conclusion

Key Takeaways

1

Greedy-choice property
A suitable local choice can be part of a globally optimal solution.

2

Optimal substructure
An optimal solution can be built from optimal subproblem solutions.

3

Proof matters
Examples help explain an algorithm, but a proof establishes why it works.

A good algorithm does not just find an answer. It has a clear reason why that answer is correct.
